From Wimbledon to £200 million leisure empire — David Lloyd has spent over five decades building spaces where people can be their best. Now he's turning that vision to where people live.

The journey

From the court to the
boardroom and beyond

1948

Born to play

David Alan Lloyd was born on 3 January 1948 in Leigh-on-Sea, Essex. Growing up in Westcliff-on-Sea near Southend, he and his brothers Tony and John began playing tennis at Westcliff Hard LTC, where their parents were members. It was the beginning of a lifelong relationship with sport.

1965

A champion in the making

At just 17, David won both the singles and doubles titles at the British Junior Championships held at Wimbledon — announcing himself as one of the most promising young players in the country. The following year he was runner-up in the junior singles, losing the final to future rugby legend J.P.R. Williams.

1970s

Davis Cup glory

David became one of Britain's most decorated tennis players of the era. He was a member of the Great Britain Davis Cup squad from 1972 to 1980, and in 1978 was part of the first British team to reach the Davis Cup final since 1937 — a historic achievement for British tennis. In 1976, he and his brother John won a doubles title in London together.

1981

Retiring from the tour

David retired from professional tennis having reached a career-high singles ranking of No. 128 in the world. But far from stepping back, his retirement marked the beginning of his most ambitious chapter yet. Following a spell coaching at a tennis club in Canada, he returned to Britain with a bold new vision.

1982

Building an empire

David founded David Lloyd Leisure, opening his first club in Heston, near Hounslow in west London. The concept was simple but revolutionary for its time — a premium health and tennis club that put community and quality at its heart. It was the same ethos he had lived by on the court, now applied to everyday life.

1992

Floated on the London Stock Exchange

By 1992, David Lloyd Leisure was a household name, and the business was floated on the London Stock Exchange. Three years later, with 18 clubs across the country, Whitbread Plc acquired the business for a reported £200 million — one of the landmark leisure deals of the decade. David remained as managing director until 1996.

1995

Coaching Britain's best

Alongside his business ventures, David was appointed British Davis Cup captain in 1995 and went on to coach Tim Henman — one of British tennis's most beloved figures — demonstrating that his passion for the sport never dimmed.

2007

Next Generation and a £925 million deal

David, together with his son Scott, created Next Generation fitness clubs — a new premium fitness concept. In 2007, London & Regional Properties, in partnership with Bank of Scotland, acquired David Lloyd Leisure from Whitbread and incorporated Next Generation into the group in a deal worth £925 million, cementing David's legacy as one of Britain's great leisure entrepreneurs.

2010s

Expanding internationally

David developed the acclaimed Sugar Hill Resort in Barbados, followed by involvement in a luxury estate in Phuket, Thailand, and the development of a villa near Marbella, Spain. His eye for quality environments and his instinct for what people want from a place to call home began to shape his next great vision.

Now

A new kind of home

Today, David Lloyd is channelling everything he has learned — about community, wellness, design and what makes people thrive — into his most personal project yet. By David Lloyd is a new generation of managed residential buildings, bringing the same premium standards he built his name on to the places people actually live. Two brands, one vision: homes that inspire.

The vision

The same principles.
A new chapter.

David's career has always been guided by a simple belief: the environment you're in shapes who you become. He saw it in sport — players who trained in the right facilities, surrounded by the right people, performed better. He proved it in business — members of his clubs didn't just get fitter, they built friendships, networks and habits that changed their lives.

By David Lloyd applies that same thinking to residential living. Not just a roof over your head, but a building designed to help you thrive — with the right spaces, the right community, and the right level of care built in from day one.

It is the most personal project of David's career, and the most ambitious. And it is only just beginning.
